User Experience & Graphic Communication Project

Author: Srulik Shriki

Case Scenario

Persona Profile: Miguel is a guest shopper in the Ypsilanti, MI area. He enjoys ordering pizza on Friday evenings and experimenting with local varieties. As a guest shopper, he prefers ordering online without account registration and opting for pickup to reduce delivery costs.

Core Need: Because Miguel is a full-time student working a part-time job, price transparency and clear reward breakdowns are essential. He needs to know exactly what he is paying for and why at every step of the transaction.

FIRST STEP

Choosing the Branch Location

Issue: Lack of Information

Scenario: Selecting the branch location. There is only one explicit instance where the user can view the full street address of the pickup branch at the start of the ordering process. In all subsequent screens, the UI displays only the city name, state, and ZIP code—omitting the street name and building number.

User Friction: When Miguel ordered as a guest user, he couldn't verify the exact store address for pickup. This forced him to make an unnecessary phone call to the store immediately after placing his order.

Operational Impact: When calling, Miguel attempted to provide an order reference, but no order number was present on his receipt. The store employee had to read through active screen orders line-by-line, matching item lists and price totals manually to identify his order.

*Screenshot taken from Jet’s Pizza website.
SECOND STEP

Choosing My Meal

Issue: Clarity

Scenario: Miguel selects the "Large Combo" item.

  • Visual Representation: The item photo displays three items next to the title "Large Combo" (pizza, breadsticks, and a drink) priced at $25.99.
  • Text Description: The description reads: "Large Pizza with Premium Mozzarella & 1 Topping, Choice of Bread, & a 2-Liter Coca-Cola Product $25.99."
  • Ambiguity: The description creates uncertainty regarding whether a bread side is included. The phrasing "Choice of Bread" easily reads as a standalone bread side rather than an option to select pizza dough type.

THIRD STEP

Building Your Own - Building the Pizza

Issue: Price Clarity

Scenario: The base Large Combo includes Premium Mozzarella cheese, 1 topping, and crust selection within the total combo cost. Additional toppings incur an extra fee.

Miguel selects pepperoni and jalapeños. During selection, the UI displays positive incremental charges for both meat and veggie additions (+$1.98 × 2). The included drink is also displayed with an added charge indicator (+1.30).

Confusion: Because the combo explicitly includes 1 topping and a drink, seeing `+$1.98` twice and `+$1.30` leads Miguel to assume the final price will be higher than advertised. This triggers confusion and anxiety during checkout.

Suggestion: Price Transparency

To communicate value without inducing sticker shock, display base-included items as `$0` or use explicit negative discount indicators (e.g., *"Discounted combo -$1.30"* or *"Included in combo ($0)"*). Reserve positive modifiers (`+`) strictly for extra add-ons beyond the combo package.

*Modified suggestion showing discount alignment.
Issue: Orientation & Navigation Comfort

A standalone chevron arrow (`>`) is used to modify crust options without descriptive text labeling.

Suggestion: Navigation Clarity

Add descriptive action copy alongside the arrow (e.g., *"Change bread choice here"*). Additionally, clarify topping costs directly by visually separating the included first topping from the charged second topping.

*Suggested UI navigation.

Crust Selection Scenario: Clicking the crust selection dropdown leads to additional user hesitation:

  • Uncertainty whether modifying crust style incurs an added fee.
  • Ambiguity regarding the primary CTA destination ("Save Options").
  • Poor placement of the "Go-back" arrow, positioned far from the focal area.
Proposed Interface Enhancements
  1. Display `$0` next to standard crust options to confirm inclusion.
  2. Add explicit text: *"First topping discounted -$1.98"*.
  3. Update CTA copy to: *"Save Options & Proceed Your Order"* to confirm progress retention.
  4. Remove confusing/redundant back links that suggest starting over.
*Redesigned interface addressing orientation, feedback, and pricing clarity.
FINAL STEP

Receipt - It’s All About TRUST!

For Miguel, the post-purchase receipt serves as a primary verification artifact. It must clearly display an order summary, price breakdown, unique order number, store address, and brand markers to instill confidence.

Key Usability Breaches Identified

Branding: Lack of brand logo and styling makes emailed receipts look untrustworthy or like spam.

Organization: Linear vertical list formatting creates heavy scanning friction; missing order number and client name hinder store pickup operations.

Unclarity:

  • Incomplete branch address ("Jet's Pizza Ypsilanti, MI 48197 MI-175").
  • Inconsistent base pricing display ($31.93 initial price vs. $25.99 combo base).
  • Omitted line items for extra topping charges.
  • Confusing discount arithmetic (-$3.96 deduction).
  • Cash payment selections display a total charged amount of `$0` without explicit "Pay at Store" status.
